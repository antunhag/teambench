# Literatura Científica Acadêmica sobre Análise de Desempenho/Jogo no Futsal

## What performance indicators/variables appear repeatedly across futsal notational-analysis studies?

### Takeaway
Across the futsal notational-analysis literature, the recurring indicator set clusters into finishing/shot outcomes (goals, shots on/off target, blocked/intercepted shots), ball-recovery and defensive actions (interceptions, tackles/desarmes, steals), passing accuracy, and the "method of play" that produced a goal or shot (positional attack, counter-attack, set piece, goalkeeper-as-outfield-player play) — but the field is methodologically fragmented, with no agreed vocabulary across countries.

### Cited Findings
- A 2016 systematic review (Agras, Ferragut & Abraldes, *International Journal of Performance Analysis in Sport*, 16(2): 652–686) screened 722 documents and retained 79 futsal match-analysis studies: 65 used notational analysis, 13 studied movement/interpersonal coordination, and 1 was itself a review. The review's main conclusion is that researchers do not agree on vocabulary or on definitions of game variables across countries, that Portuguese is the dominant publication language (limiting international dissemination), and that only ~13% of studies appeared in international journals — [Source](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/rpanxx/v16y2016i2p652-686.html).
- A separate review of 50 articles on technical-tactical performance analysis in futsal found technical actions accounted for ~65.97% of analysed actions, tactical actions ~19.75%, goalkeeper actions ~8.40%, and set pieces ~5.88% of the actions studied, and recommended more research on women's futsal, goalkeepers, and defensive actions — [Source](https://rsdjournal.org/rsd/article/view/20450).
- A PRISMA-based review of 27 studies on multivariate statistics in futsal competition analysis found 55.6% of studies addressed technical-tactical aspects, 33.3% physical aspects, and 11.1% psychological aspects, and called for more methodological rigor/standardization — [Source](https://portalinvestigacion.um.es/documentos/67dc6ca9b4628536497c3403?lang=en).
- A Brazilian study (Revista Brasileira de Futsal e Futebol, 2024) coded offensive technical indicators (goals, shots saved/defended, shots off target, shots blocked/intercepted) and defensive indicators (tackles/desarmes, interceptions, ball steals/roubadas de bola) across 56 matches; teams with better final standings had statistically more goals and more "shots defended" (goalkeeper saves), with large effect sizes — [Source](https://doaj.org/article/49d0c98733634dfa8e95daec48b45f3b).
- A longitudinal study (Tourinho Filho, Soares & Barbanti) filmed 10 players from a professional Rio Grande do Sul team across a season, coding correct/incorrect passes, tackles (desarmes), shots, interceptions, assists, and ball losses; correct-pass rate and assists dropped after the first phase of the championship while other indicators stayed stable — [Source](https://efdeportes.com/efd141/desempenho-em-quadra-de-jogadores-de-futsal.htm).
- A descriptive study of the AFC Women's Futsal Championship 2025 (26 matches, 12 national teams, BEPRO Analysis Software) tracked seven attacking indicators — goals, assists, total shots, shots on target, shooting accuracy, shots off target, blocked shots — and found goals correlated strongly with assists, total shots, and shots on target, but not with shooting accuracy; authors recommend adding defensive and contextual variables in future work — [Source](https://ejournal.upi.edu/index.php/tegar/article/view/91162).
- An international comparison of futsal national teams (Dogramaci et al., 2015) found the Brazilian team had the highest ball possession and the highest pass-success rate (93%), while Australian players covered greater relative high-intensity/sprint distance than Spanish players — [Source](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/bitstream/10453/137965/1/Dogramaci%20et%20al%202015%20IJES%20Activity%20Profile%20Differences%20in%20Futsal.pdf).
- A UTS doctoral thesis validated an event-recording (notational) system for tracking futsal movement patterns, concluding notational tracking is valid/reliable and may be preferable to GPS for indoor sports like futsal — [Source](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/handle/10453/36843).
- A Croatian first-division goalkeeper study (2015/16 season) used notational video analysis to track saves, save efficiency, save types, and distribution; goalkeepers averaged 13.13 ± 5.8 saves per match with ~79.6% average efficiency — [Source](https://www.bib.irb.hr:8443/1214199).
- A professional-futsal multilevel study of 260 matches and >3,700 shots found average goalkeeper save percentage of 84.3 ± 10.0%; match-level factors (competition level, location, outfield defensive actions) were not significantly associated with save %, but shot context was — rebounds, penalties, and direct free kicks raised concession probability, while long-distance shots lowered it — [Source](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/determinants-of-goalkeeper-defensive-performance-in-professional-futsal-a-multilevel-analysis-of-match-and-shot-contexts/).

### Inferences
- Because 65/79 studies in the main systematic review are notational and Portuguese is the dominant language, the strongest body of granular, "data points coders actually track" work is in Brazilian/Portuguese academic journals (Revista Brasileira de Futsal e Futebol, EFDeportes, dissertations), not in the indexed English-language journals — any TeamBench indicator taxonomy should draw heavily on these Lusophone sources rather than only Journal of Sports Sciences-type papers.
- The repeated appearance of "shots defended/saved" as a team-level success indicator (not just a goalkeeper individual stat) suggests futsal research treats goalkeeper save counts as part of the *team's* offensive-pressure/defensive-resilience profile, not purely an isolated GK metric.
- The consistent recommendation across multiple reviews ("more research needed on defensive actions, goalkeepers, women's futsal") signals that defensive/goalkeeper indicator sets are less standardized than offensive/shot indicators — a taxonomy should expect more "home-grown" categories for these.

### Gaps
- No single canonical "core indicator list" (akin to a validated notational instrument with named variables) was found in the sources retrieved; the 2016 systematic review's full text — which likely tabulates the specific variables used per study — is paywalled, so only its abstract/conclusions were accessible.
- Precise operational definitions (e.g., exact difference between "shot blocked" vs "shot intercepted" vs "shot off target") were not confirmed across sources and may vary by study.

## How do studies typically categorize shot outcomes and shot locations on a futsal court (what granularity of zones is standard)?

### Takeaway
There is no single standardized shot-zone grid in futsal research; studies use anywhere from ~6 to 9 court zones (often dividing the attacking half into finishing sub-zones), and consistently cross shot zone with shot type/technique and contact surface (foot, which surface of the foot, head, etc.) as correlated variables, following observation instruments adapted from football (e.g., SOF-CODER).

### Cited Findings
- Lapresa et al. (2013, *Journal of Sports Sciences*, 31(15): 1731–1739, DOI 10.1080/02640414.2013.803584) coded every shot taken by the Spanish national futsal team at the 2010 UEFA Futsal Championship (which Spain won), using SOF-CODER, a football observation instrument adapted to futsal. They found a significant association between shooting zone and shot type (χ² = 55.821, p = 0.006) and between contact surface and shot type (χ² = 30.293, p = 0.017); temporal pattern analysis (T-patterns) of the zone/surface/shot-type relationship enabled a tactical interpretation of the futsal offensive phase — [Source](https://investigacion.unirioja.es/documentos/5bbc69a3b750603269e81eca?lang=en); preprint at [Source](https://reunir.unir.net/bitstream/123456789/4810/2/observational_analysis_of_the_offensive_sequences_that_ended_preprint.pdf).
- An observational study of 2,449 goals in the Spanish professional futsal league (2012–2014) tested associations among shot zone, contact surface, shot type, play type (method of play), number of passes, and number of players involved; most goals came from fast plays with one or two passes — [Source](https://zaguan.unizar.es/record/70195/files/texto_completo.pdf?version=1).
- A national youth championship study coded goals by finishing zone using 9 pre-defined court zones and by match half; the share of goals conceded in "Zone 5" varied sharply by age category (13.41% at U17 vs. 2.43% at a senior/S15 category), and authors cautioned against generalizing zone-specific figures across age groups — [Source](https://ubibliorum.ubi.pt/handle/10400.6/15060).
- Bolsonaro (2015) analyzed finishing actions across six court sectors/zones, treating shot location as a central offensive-performance indicator — [Source](https://efdeportes.com/efdeportes/index.php/EFDeportes/article/download/8008/2626?inline=1) (via search summary; cf. DOAJ record [Source](https://doaj.org/article/080ee78dfe614aedb629a0f3a824ba15)).
- Sarmento et al. (2016) analyzed 30 Spanish Primera División de Sala matches with computerized notation and T-pattern detection; they found central court zones were the most common origin of shots, and positional attack produced the most goals (42%), followed by set pieces (27%) — DOI 10.1080/02640414.2015.1066024 — [Source](https://oasisbr.ibict.br/vufind/Record/RCAP_5a3401cba6d8587b18a97d3f06d34efd).

### Inferences
- The recurring pairing of "zone × shot type × contact surface" (rather than zone alone) implies that a useful futsal shot-tracking schema should capture at least three linked fields per shot event: location zone, technique/type (e.g., finta, chute de bico, cavadinha — not enumerated in the abstracts retrieved), and striking surface, since these are the variables studies statistically associate with each other.
- The variation in zone granularity (6 zones vs. 9 zones) across studies, and explicit researcher warnings against generalizing zone-outcome percentages across age/competition levels, suggests zone schemes are usually built per-study rather than adopted from one universal standard — a product like TeamBench has latitude to define its own zone grid as long as it's internally consistent and documented.

### Gaps
- The exact zone boundaries/labels (e.g., the specific layout of the "9 zones" in the youth championship study, or the "6 sectors" in Bolsonaro 2015) were not retrievable from search snippets/abstracts alone; full-text access would be needed to reproduce an exact diagram.
- The precise list of shot-type/technique categories (e.g., instep, outside-of-foot, chip, bicycle kick, header) used in the Lapresa et al. (2013) SOF-CODER adaptation was not confirmed verbatim (full PDF fetch failed due to a connection error).

## What offensive-phase and defensive-phase indicators are used (organized attack vs. fast attack/counter-attack, pressing types, defensive system descriptors)?

### Takeaway
Futsal studies consistently split goal/attack origin into categories such as ataque posicional (positional/organized attack vs. an organized defense, typically 4×4), contra-ataque (counter-attack, from ball recovery to a quick finish before the defense reorganizes), bola parada (set pieces), and jogo do goleiro-linha (flying-goalkeeper/5th-attacker play); within counter-attacks they further code the recovery action, start zone, number of players/passes involved, and outcome, while defensive-phase description relies more on coaching-literature system labels (e.g., mid-block) than on standardized notational variables.

### Cited Findings
- In women's futsal, positional attack was the most frequent goal-scoring method, followed by counter-attack and set pieces, with defenses failing most often against positional attack — [Source](https://portalrevistas.ucb.br/index.php/rbcm/article/download/4049/8582).
- Another study found a near-even split among four goal-origin categories: positional attack 24.3%, counter-attack 24.3%, set piece 23.1%, and goleiro-linha (flying-goalkeeper) play 21.8% — [Source](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/4901928.pdf) (cf. related records at [Source](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/4901942.pdf) and [Source](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/4901989.pdf)).
- A Liga Nacional de Futsal (Brazil) 2017 second-phase goal-origin study found set pieces were the most frequent origin (31%), ahead of counter-attack (21%) and positional attack (19%); within set-piece goals, most followed a single pass exchange before the finish, with the unmarked ("sem barreira") direct free kick being common — [Source](https://repositorio.ufsc.br/handle/123456789/187758).
- A different sample reported counter-attack as the dominant method (37.25%) ahead of positional attack (30.85%), illustrating that the ranking of attack-origin categories varies by competition/sample — [Source](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/4901919.pdf).
- Counter-attack is operationally defined in this literature as the phase starting at ball recovery, in which the team transitions from defender to attacker and seeks to finish before the opposing defense reorganizes; positional attack is defined as attacking against an organized defense, typically shaped 4×4 — [Source](https://revistas.ufg.br/fef/article/view/24662).
- Defensive transition is described as running from loss of possession to the moment the team stabilizes (or fails to stabilize) against the opponent's counter-attack, with defensive return-run (retorno defensivo) and delaying play (temporização) as its main components — [Source](https://periodicos.ufsc.br/index.php/motrivivencia/article/download/85835/51146/335568).
- A study of 1,043 counter-attacks in the Brazilian league (2019/20 season) found direct ball recovery was the dominant initiating action (86.6% of cases); 53.3% of counter-attacks ended with no shot, 40.7% ended with a shot, and 6.0% ended in a goal — [Source](https://revistas.ufg.br/fef/article/download/142/1523?inline=1).
- Other studies additionally code the counter-attack's start zone on the court, the number of players involved, and the specific defensive action that generated it (interception, tackle/desarme, block, or goalkeeper save) — [Source](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/4901928.pdf) (synthesis of multiple cited sub-indices).
- A tactical-schemes case study (Ferreira et al., 2018, *Human Movement*) applied notational, sequential, and T-pattern analysis to one professional team's out-of-play (throw-in/corner) tactical schemes using a dedicated "Tactical Scheme Observation System in Futsal," finding finishing often followed a pass into outer/wide zones — [Source](https://www.termedia.pl/Notational-sequential-and-T-patterns-analysis-of-the-tactical-schemes-of-a-professional-Futsal-team,132,34166,0,1.html).
- Set-piece goals in the Liga Nacional de Futsal 2024 (346 set-piece goals coded) were categorized into throw-in (lateral), corner kick (escanteio), foul/free kick (falta), penalty (pênalti), and direct free kick (tiro livre); corners accounted for ~32% and free kicks ~24%, throw-ins ~23% of these set-piece goals — [Source](https://static.even3.com/anais/1337492.pdf) (via search synthesis; see also [Source](https://ojs.fiepbulletin.net/fiepbulletin/article/download/6397/54615894/54616409)).
- A single-team case study (UFSM, 2019; 8 matches) coded 95 throw-in actions and 43 corner-kick actions; only corner kicks produced goals in that small sample — [Source](https://revistas.unipar.br/index.php/saude/article/download/3415/2317/11102).
- On the defensive-system side, a FIFA coaching resource (not a peer-reviewed notational study, but a frequently cited practitioner framework) describes the mid-block as the most commonly deployed defensive system in futsal, intended to keep the ball far enough from goal to limit direct shooting opportunities — [Source](https://fifatrainingcentre.com/en/practice/futsal/block-3/defending-in-a-mid-block.php).
- The 2016 systematic review and the Indonesian AFC study both explicitly flag that defensive and contextual variables (pressing type, defensive system descriptors) are under-represented relative to offensive/technical variables in the existing futsal literature — [Source](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/rpanxx/v16y2016i2p652-686.html); [Source](https://ejournal.upi.edu/index.php/tegar/article/view/91162).

### Inferences
- Because "goal/attack origin" (positional / counter-attack / set piece / goleiro-linha) is the most consistently used top-level categorical indicator across many independent Brazilian/Spanish/Portuguese studies — even though the relative percentages differ by competition — this four-category taxonomy is a strong candidate for a standard "attack type" field in any futsal match-coding schema.
- Within counter-attacks, the recurring sub-indicators (recovery type, start zone, players/passes involved, outcome) suggest that counter-attacks deserve a richer sub-schema than other attack types, reflecting their outsized tactical importance in futsal.
- Pressing-type and defensive-system descriptors (e.g., man-marking vs. zone, high press vs. mid-block vs. low block) appear far more often in coaching/practitioner sources than in peer-reviewed notational studies — any defensive-system field in a data model will likely need to borrow vocabulary from coaching literature rather than a validated academic instrument.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed futsal-specific notational study with a validated pressing-intensity or defensive-system-coding instrument (analogous to the football high-press studies found) was located; the closest analogues retrieved were football studies (e.g., Journal of Sports Sciences 2021 on high-press vs. deep defending in youth football) and a practitioner (FIFA) resource, not futsal-specific peer-reviewed notational work.
- Exact definitions distinguishing "pressing" sub-types (e.g., orientated pressing, double press) in a futsal-specific academic coding scheme were not found in the retrieved sources.

## Are there indicators specific to futsal's 5-a-side, rolling-substitution, flying-goalkeeper (goleiro-linha) structure that wouldn't appear in 11-a-side football research?

### Takeaway
Yes — the "goleiro-linha"/power-play (flying goalkeeper acting as a fifth attacker, creating a 5v4 in possession) is a distinct, well-documented futsal-specific attacking method with its own goal-origin category and risk/reward framing (extra attacker vs. empty-goal exposure), and it appears as a dedicated statistical category in Brazilian goal-origin studies alongside positional attack, counter-attack, and set pieces.

### Cited Findings
- The futsal "power play" is defined as the goalkeeper joining the attack as an extra outfield player, creating a 5-outfield-player/4-defender numerical situation in possession; teams typically use it when trailing late in a match — [Source](https://futsal.tech/en/special/pp); Brazilian-Portuguese term "goleiro-linha," described as a "fifth man" joining the outfield players — [Source](https://www.5-a-side.com/futsal/futsal-fly-goalkeepers-rules-and-strategy-explained/).
- Goal-origin studies in Brazilian futsal explicitly include "jogo do goleiro-linha" as one of the four standard attack-origin categories, accounting for as much as 21.8% of goals in one dataset and roughly 8% of goals in another competition dataset — [Source](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/4901928.pdf); [Source](https://educapes.capes.gov.br/handle/capes/1024090?mode=full).
- Tactical write-ups of elite European teams describe specific power-play patterns: an own-half kick-in with the goalkeeper as the fifth player in a 3-2 shape, routing the ball to a pivot on the front line or back to the goalkeeper when the "minus-support" player is pressed; a double press from the opponent is identified as the main risk factor making it unsafe for the goalkeeper to advance — [Source](https://en.futsal.tech/?p=4825); a companion piece lists the tactic's core advantage (persistent numerical edge) and core disadvantage (becoming trapped against a team with prepared defensive guidelines) — [Source](https://en.futsal.tech/?p=4841).
- One review notes that the theoretical attacking advantage of the 5v4+Goalkeeper tactic has been little studied empirically, and that existing evidence suggests effectiveness lower than theoretically expected — [Source](https://docta.ucm.es/entities/publication/855367e2-2cbc-4bc0-84fa-b1431f020acf) (abstract excerpt; full text not retrieved).
- Related futsal-specific set-piece subtype: the free kick "without a wall/barrier" (tiro livre sem barreira) — a consequence of futsal's accumulated-fouls rule rather than a standard football wall — appears as its own coded category in set-piece goal-origin studies — [Source](https://efdeportes.com/efd184/tiro-livre-direto-sem-barreira-em-futsal.htm); also noted as a frequent sub-type within set-piece goals in the Liga Nacional de Futsal 2017 analysis — [Source](https://repositorio.ufsc.br/handle/123456789/187758).

### Inferences
- The consistent presence of "goleiro-linha" as a top-level, named attack-origin category (not folded into "positional attack" or treated as a rare edge case) confirms this is a structurally distinct, independently tracked indicator specific to futsal — a data model should treat it as its own attack-type value rather than a modifier on positional attack.
- The "free kick without a wall" set-piece subtype is a direct artifact of futsal's accumulated team-foul rule (after 5 team fouls per half, opponents get a free kick from the second penalty spot with no defensive wall allowed) — this is a futsal-specific set-piece category with no 11-a-side football equivalent and should be a distinct set-piece sub-type in any schema.
- No direct academic evidence was found tying rolling/unlimited substitutions specifically to a named notational indicator (e.g., "substitution frequency" or "shift length" as a tracked tactical variable in the match-analysis literature, as opposed to physical/fitness time-motion research) — this appears to be an under-researched area relative to goleiro-linha and set pieces.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm empirical effectiveness statistics (e.g., goals scored vs. conceded rate) specifically for the goleiro-linha/power-play tactic beyond the ~8–21.8% share-of-goals figures noted above; the review suggesting "lower than expected effectiveness" was only available as an abstract excerpt, not full text.
- No academic notational-analysis source was found that treats futsal's rolling substitution system itself (as opposed to flying-goalkeeper tactics) as a coded match-analysis indicator (e.g., substitution timing, line changes, or "quinteto" rotations) — this may be a genuine research gap rather than a search-coverage gap, consistent with the broader finding that tactical/contextual variables are under-studied relative to technical/shot indicators.

## What indicators relate to numerical superiority/inferiority situations (e.g. power-plays, transitions with a player advantage)?

### Takeaway
The main peer-reviewed study on this topic (Gómez et al., 2019, Spanish professional league) analyzed attacks occurring after an opponent's player was sent off/dismissed (numerical-superiority attacks), finding attacking efficiency depended heavily on match context — home advantage, specific time windows, and the number of fouls already accumulated by the defending team — and identified four distinct cluster-derived "types" of superiority attacks; dedicated academic research on numerical-inferiority (defending a man down) situations specifically was not found.

### Cited Findings
- Gómez et al. (*Journal of Sports Sciences*, 2019) analyzed 125 attacking game situations with numerical superiority (following an opponent dismissal) from a regular season of the Spanish professional Futsal League — [Source](https://docta.ucm.es/entities/publication/855367e2-2cbc-4bc0-84fa-b1431f020acf).
- Logistic regression found the highest attacking efficiency when the attacking team played at home, the attack occurred during minutes 33–36 of the match, and the opposing (defending) team already had 3 fouls accumulated — [Source](https://docta.ucm.es/entities/publication/855367e2-2cbc-4bc0-84fa-b1431f020acf).
- A two-step cluster analysis identified four distinct types of attack under numerical-superiority conditions — [Source](https://docta.ucm.es/entities/publication/855367e2-2cbc-4bc0-84fa-b1431f020acf).
- Contextual variables ranked by influence in the model were: match type, match status (score situation), and the attacking team's own fouls (highest influence), followed by match location and attack duration (lower influence) — [Source](https://docta.ucm.es/entities/publication/855367e2-2cbc-4bc0-84fa-b1431f020acf).
- The authors frame the practical application as helping coaches design and train for superiority/inferiority scenarios and monitor them live during matches, implying inferiority situations are the trained counterpart even though this study's data covers only the superiority side — [Source](https://docta.ucm.es/entities/publication/855367e2-2cbc-4bc0-84fa-b1431f020acf).
- Separately, the goleiro-linha/power-play tactic (see previous section) is itself a self-induced numerical-superiority situation in possession (5 outfield-equivalent attackers vs. 4 defenders), and is tracked as a distinct goal-origin category in Brazilian studies, with reported effectiveness "lower than theoretically expected" per one review abstract — [Source](https://en.futsal.tech/?p=4825); [Source](https://docta.ucm.es/entities/publication/855367e2-2cbc-4bc0-84fa-b1431f020acf).

### Inferences
- Because the only dedicated empirical study found used "opponent dismissal" (red card / expulsion) as the trigger for numerical superiority, the futsal-specific accumulated-foul rule (which produces a different kind of "advantage" via direct free kicks rather than a sustained player advantage) appears to be treated as a separate indicator category (set pieces / free kick without wall) rather than merged with numerical-superiority attacks in this literature — a data schema should keep "numerical superiority from an opponent being sent off" distinct from "foul-accumulation consequences" (penalty-spot free kicks).
- The finding that fouls-already-accumulated-by-the-defense and match time-window both predict superiority-attack efficiency suggests any numerical-superiority indicator in a coding schema should be paired with contextual fields (score state, elapsed time, opponent's foul count) rather than coded as a standalone binary flag.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed futsal source specifically studying numerical-inferiority (defending while a player short) situations as their own analytical category was found in this search; the Gómez et al. (2019) study and related abstracts cover only the superiority/attacking side.
- The four cluster-derived "attack types" under numerical superiority were mentioned by the abstract but not enumerated by name in the retrieved text (full paper not fetched) — the specific definitions of these four types remain unknown from the sources gathered.
- Quantitative outcome data (e.g., scoring rate per numerical-superiority possession, or per power-play possession) comparing superiority situations to normal play was not found/confirmed in the sources retrieved.
