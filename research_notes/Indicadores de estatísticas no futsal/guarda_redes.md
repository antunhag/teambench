# Futsal Goalkeeper-Specific Match Statistics and Indicators

## What shot-stopping indicators are tracked for futsal goalkeepers (saves by type/zone, goals conceded by type/zone, 1-on-1 won/lost)?

### Takeaway
Futsal-specific research tracks save/goal outcomes mainly through three lenses — save percentage at the match level, shot-context variables (distance, height, zone, whether the GK intervened inside or outside the penalty area), and save **technique/body-part taxonomies** (hands vs. feet, with/without falling) — but no source found reports a standardized "1-on-1 won/lost" indicator specific to futsal; that remains a coaching-practice concept rather than a documented statistic.

### Cited Findings
- A multilevel study of professional futsal (260 matches; 3,725 saves and 675 goals at the shot level) found an average goalkeeper save percentage of 84.3% ± 10.0%, and that goalkeeper interventions **outside the penalty area** were associated with substantially lower odds of a goal (OR = 0.33, p < 0.001); goal probability was also linked to shot distance, shot height, and goalkeeper positioning (medium/long-distance shots → lower scoring odds; high shots → higher scoring odds) — [Determinants of goalkeeper defensive performance in professional futsal (DOI 10.1177/17479541261462904)](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/determinants-of-goalkeeper-defensive-performance-in-professional-futsal-a-multilevel-analysis-of-match-and-shot-contexts/)
- In a sample of 31 female state-level futsal matches (UFMA, Brazil), of 698 total goalkeeper saves: saves with the hands = 464 (66.48%), saves with the feet = 75 (10.74%), and saves on goal-exits/claims = 159 (22.78%) — [search result summary, UFMA futsal feminino study](https://doaj.org/article/e5f3765926b847df87e150d82b46c71b) (full text not independently verified — see Gaps)
- In a men's futsal study (Blumenau), the most-used save **technique** was "without falling" (sem queda) at 37.41% of defensive actions, followed by "falling using the feet" (queda com os pés) at 15.11%; the dataset recorded 170 shots on target faced, 31 goals conceded, and 139 defensive actions — [search result summary, Blumenau men's futsal study](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/6112202.pdf)
- A youth futsal scouting study (2022 Parintins School Games, Brazil) recorded goalkeeper saves by shot zone and by a numbered "goal location" grid; the keeper was most effective at goal-location zone "8", saving 94.12% of shots directed there, and the most common save types were "low save without falling" and "medium-height save without falling" — [Análise das ações técnicas dos goleiros de futsal — Parintins 2022](https://riu.ufam.edu.br/handle/prefix/8327)
- A study of goalkeepers acting as outfield players in elite futsal found that greater shooting chances for the opposing team arose in balanced matches during playoffs and when the attacking team had committed no more than two fouls, while negative effects (more risk) appeared during the regular season and in the final moments of matches — [Goalkeeper as an outfield player: shooting chances at critical moments in elite futsal](https://lida.sport-iat.de/dfb/Record/4063433?lng=en)

### Inferences
- The field appears to be converging on **shot context** (distance, height, in-box vs. out-of-box location of the GK) as the primary explanatory variable for save success, rather than isolated GK attributes — suggesting that any internal indicator set for teambench should pair "save/goal conceded" events with shot-origin zone and GK starting position, not just a save tally.
- No source uses the term "1-on-1" as a formal futsal statistic; it is likely folded into general save/goal-conceded counts in the academic literature, even though it is a common coaching concept.

### Gaps
- No source gives a standardized, position-agnostic "shot map" (e.g., 6 or 9 zones) specifically validated for futsal goalkeepers the way such maps exist for 11-a-side football; the zone systems found (e.g., the Parintins "goal location 8") are study-specific, informally defined, and not independently verifiable from the search snippet alone (full text not fetched).
- No source reports a formal 1-on-1 (breakaway) won/lost indicator for futsal goalkeepers.
- The UFMA save-type breakdown (66.48% hands / 10.74% feet / 22.78% goal-exit) could not be cross-checked against the full paper (only the search-engine's summary of the source was available); treat the exact percentages as provisional.

## What distribution/restart indicators matter (throws/rolls to start counter-attacks, long distribution, "reposição de bola")?

### Takeaway
The only quantitative futsal-specific study found measured goalkeeper throw-distribution effectiveness for launching counter-attacks and found it strikingly low (under 4% success); qualitative sources (FIFA, coaching literature) consistently frame the GK's restart/distribution as the "first pass" of the attack and a key decision-speed skill, but hard per-match indicators (distribution accuracy %, long vs. short distribution split, time-to-release) were not found as standardized futsal statistics.

### Cited Findings
- A study of the 2018 Professional Futsal League Final Four (Indonesia) measured the effectiveness of goalkeeper throw distribution in generating counter-attacks and found a 3.85% success rate versus a 96.15% failure rate, attributing failures to defensive readiness and to technical errors (poor ball control, inaccurate throws) — [Efektivitas distribusi lemparan kiper terhadap hasil counter attack, Final Four Liga Futsal Profesional 2018](https://repository.upi.edu/48514)
- FIFA's coverage of the FIFA Futsal World Cup notes that pressing and high defensive blocks have pushed goalkeepers toward more direct distribution, and that the speed of the goalkeeper's decision-making when distributing is described as key to starting an attack — [FIFA Training Centre: Goalkeeper distribution, FIFA Futsal World Cup 2021](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/game/tournaments/fifa-futsal-world-cup/goalkeeper-distribution.php)
- A coaching resource states the futsal goalkeeper is effectively "the first attacker" and must be accurate over both short and long distances with both hands and feet, including receiving the ball under pressure — [British Football Coaches: What should you know about futsal](https://www.britishfootballcoaches.com/post/what-should-you-know-about-futsal)
- The 2025 UEFA Under-19 Futsal EURO technical report noted the widespread use of direct goalkeeper throws into the opposition half specifically to break opponent pressure, as part of describing that "modern goalkeepers must contribute with the ball as well as defend the goal" — [UEFA: 2025 Under-19 Futsal EURO technical report (news summary)](https://www.uefa.com/futsalunder19/news/029f-1f2b7c2a6953-300acec35114-1000--read-the-2025-uefa-under-19-futsal-euro-technical-report/)

### Inferences
- The very low success rate (3.85%) found in the one quantitative study suggests that "successful direct counter-attack off a GK throw" is a rare, high-value event in futsal — if teambench wants a meaningful GK distribution indicator, raw attempt counts may be more useful than success-rate alone, given how rare clean conversions appear to be.
- The specific Brazilian/Portuguese term "reposição de bola" (ball restart/goal-kick-equivalent after a goal or dead ball) did not surface a dedicated statistical study; it is discussed in rule-history terms (how many touches/where it can be received) more than as a tracked performance indicator.

### Gaps
- No source provides a breakdown of long vs. short distribution (hand throw vs. foot pass vs. rolled ball) with associated outcome rates in professional/elite futsal — only the Indonesian Final Four study, which is a small sample (one tournament, "Final Four" = likely 4 teams) and focused narrowly on throws leading to counter-attacks.
- No source quantifies "reposição de bola" (the futsal goal-kick/restart equivalent) as a distinct tracked indicator separate from general distribution.
- Could not fetch johk.pl's "Goalkeeper Influence on Ball Possession Effectiveness in Futsal" (certificate error on fetch) — this may have contained relevant possession/distribution metrics; flagged as an unexplored source.

## What indicators exist specifically for the "goleiro-linha" (flying goalkeeper) tactic — numeric-advantage situations, and what happens statistically when it goes wrong (empty-net goals, "baliza deserta")?

### Takeaway
Brazilian/Iberian academic literature treats "goleiro-linha" (GK plays as auxiliary attacker, keeping the GK role/shirt) and "linha-goleiro" (an outfield player substitutes in for the GK) as **two distinct, separately-tracked tactics** — this distinction itself is a futsal-specific indicator-design finding. Both are logged via situational triggers (score state, half, reception zone), outcome counts (shots, wrong passes, goals), and — at the professional/international level — FIFA and UEFA track deployment-frequency and goal-conversion-rate trends for the "fly goalkeeper" tactic across tournaments. No source used the literal term "baliza deserta" (empty net) for futsal; that term only surfaced in handball research.

### Cited Findings
- A study of 10 games from the 2016 Brazil Liga Nacional de Futsal (first phase) analyzed goleiro-linha (183 plays) and linha-goleiro (45 plays, across only 6 of 10 games) as separate tactics with separate indicator sets (game stage, ball-reception zone, triggering situation, scoreline, and play outcome) — [Goleiro-linha e linha-goleiro: diferentes usos estratégico-táticos do goleiro na Liga Nacional de Futsal de 2016](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/6816971.pdf)
- In that study, goleiro-linha was triggered mainly by throw-in restarts (44.83% of plays) and by opponent pressing (28.41%); linha-goleiro was triggered mainly by positional attack (42.22%), corner kicks (28.89%) and throw-ins (26.67%) — [same source](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/6816971.pdf)
- Scoreline context differed sharply: goleiro-linha was used in a roughly balanced way whether the team was losing (30.1%), tied (31.7%), or winning (38.3%); linha-goleiro, by contrast, was used **only** when the team was behind on the scoreboard — [same source](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/6816971.pdf)
- Shots were attempted in 43% of goleiro-linha plays (79 of 183) and 22% of linha-goleiro plays (10 of 45), but goal conversion was extremely low: only 1.6% of goleiro-linha plays ended in a goal, and linha-goleiro produced zero goals in the sample — the authors flag "wrong passes" and failed finishes as the dominant outcomes for both tactics, questioning the tactic's offensive efficiency as executed by these teams — [same source](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/6816971.pdf)
- At the FIFA Futsal World Cup, "fly goalkeeper" deployments rose 74.5% from 2021 to 2024, and the share of fly-goalkeeper incidents that ended in a goal rose from 1% (2021) to 5.9% (2024); FIFA's analysis separately tracked a "powerplay" indicator (deployments +38.1%, same-tournament comparison) where the **goal rate fell** from 7.6% to 4.2% and the **concede rate** (i.e., conceding into the vacated goal/structure) was 6.5% in 2024 (down from 7.6% in 2021) — teams were still more likely to concede than score off a powerplay. Powerplay attempts increasingly came from zones further from goal (Zone 4 share rose from 20.5% to 28.5%; Zone 3 from 16.3% to 18.4%) — [FIFA Training Centre: Understanding FIFA Futsal World Cup 2024 in 5 key observations](https://fifatrainingcentre.com/en/game/tournaments/ffwc/2024/understanding-fifa-futsal-world-cup-2024-in-5-key-observations.php)
- A European league study (Spanish, Russian, Italian top divisions, 2014–15 season) analyzed 673 goals scored in fly-goalkeeper situations and found the fly-goalkeeper strategy accounted for 15.33% of all goals scored across the sample, with the Italian league the only one where teams scored more using the tactic in attack than they conceded while defending against it — [Use and effectiveness of fly goalkeepers in European futsal (search-result summary)](https://zaguan.unizar.es/record/89783) (full text could not be fetched directly — connection error; relying on the search engine's summary of this primary source)
- A related Spanish-language study of the portero-jugador (goalkeeper-as-outfield-player) tactic across the same three leagues/season (4,389 regular-season goals analyzed) reported that goals scored while a team defended conventionally converted into match points in 97.9% of cases, versus only 35.1% of cases when the goal was scored while a team was attacking using the flying-goalkeeper tactic — the authors treat goals conceded during a team's own flying-goalkeeper attack as a classification-relevant risk indicator — [Análisis del portero-jugador y la clasificación en el fútbol sala europeo (search-result summary)](https://zaguan.unizar.es/record/126201) (full text returned HTTP 403 on direct fetch; exact framing of "defending" vs. "attacking" cases should be verified against the primary source before being treated as final)
- A situational-factors study of goals scored with 5v4+goalkeeper (fly-GK) found that opponent level was the most important situational variable — using the tactic against an opponent of at least similar quality was associated with scoring — and that in both of the two leading statistical clusters of fly-GK goals, the scoring team had been losing at the time; home/away location was the least important situational variable studied — [Situational and Game Conditioning Factors in Goals Scored with a Fly Goalkeeper in Futsal](https://revista-apunts.com/en/?p=35263)
- In the same study, two goal-type patterns dominated the sample: a "precision goal" (48.8% of goals: short 1–10 pass buildup, shot taken from inside the goal area, often via 1v1/pass/combination, with all 5 players involved) and a "surprise goal" (32.1%: short buildup, ending on the attack's right side, often finished with an outside-the-area shot); overall 61.7% of the 359 fly-GK goals analyzed were scored from inside the goal area — [same source](https://revista-apunts.com/en/?p=35263)
- At club level, UEFA's 2023/24 Futsal Champions League technical report highlighted goalkeepers advancing with the ball to create a numerical advantage as a recurring feature of the final (e.g., Palma's Luan Muller as a "fifth attacker"), and described defensive countermeasures such as switching to a 1-3 shape and cutting off the cross-court passing lane to force the ball-carrying goalkeeper into a low-percentage shot — [UEFA: Tactical insight — five themes from the UEFA Futsal Champions League](https://www.uefa.com/uefafutsalchampionsleague/news/0281-18065d0d7c90-e6c0ab4ae43e-1000--tactical-insight-five-themes-from-the-uefa-futsal-champion/)

### Inferences
- The FIFA World Cup "fly goalkeeper" vs. "powerplay" split suggests these may be tracked by FIFA as two related-but-distinct situational categories (possibly: GK advancing into build-up play generally vs. the formal 5-vs-4 numeric-overload substitution) — this distinction was not explicitly defined in the fetched article and should be treated as an open terminology question, not a confirmed FIFA taxonomy, before teambench adopts either term as a fixed indicator label.
- Despite small, older, and geographically narrow samples, every quantitative study found reports the same qualitative pattern: fly-goalkeeper/goleiro-linha situations produce many more shot attempts than goals, and the conversion rate is consistently low (1–6% range across studies) — this is a fairly robust cross-study finding even though no single study is large or recent enough to be definitive on its own.
- "Empty-net goal conceded while a team's GK is upfield as the extra attacker" appears to be the practical equivalent of what the task brief calls "baliza deserta," but no futsal source uses that Portuguese term or isolates that exact event as its own named statistic — the closest documented analogue is the "goals conceded during a team's own flying-goalkeeper attack" framing in the zaguan.unizar.es 126201 study (97.9% vs. 35.1% point-conversion finding above).

### Gaps
- "Baliza deserta" returned no futsal sources at all; it is an established term in handball research (for the analogous empty-net/extra-attacker tactic) but was not found applied to futsal in any indexed source. If teambench wants this exact term for internal UI, it would be introducing new nomenclature rather than following existing published usage.
- The zaguan.unizar.es sources (89783 and 126201) could not be fetched in full (socket/connection error and HTTP 403, respectively); all findings from them rely on the search engine's own summary of the source, which is a weaker form of evidence than a direct primary-source read. These should be re-verified via direct PDF download if precision matters for a published document.
- No source gives a clean, single "numeric advantage situations created" count (e.g., "team played 5v4 for N seconds/possessions") — the closest proxies are deployment counts (number of fly-GK/powerplay incidents) and goal/concession rates per incident.

## Are there indicators for a futsal GK's outfield-style actions when playing as goleiro-linha (passes, shots, as a 5th outfield player)?

### Takeaway
When acting as goleiro-linha, the GK's actions are tracked using largely the same outcome categories as any outfield player in possession (pass completion/errors, shots on/off target, ball losses, fouls for/against, even penalties for/against) layered on top of GK-specific situational tags (triggering situation, reception zone, scoreline) — but the tactic's effect on team defensive structure is also measured separately as a "defensive subsystem" indicator.

### Cited Findings
- The 2016 Liga Nacional de Futsal study tracked, as outcome categories for goleiro-linha/linha-goleiro plays: goals scored, wrong passes, total shots, shots on target, shots off target, balls lost, fouls committed, fouls suffered, penalties suffered, penalties committed, and goals conceded — treating the GK-as-attacker exactly like an outfield possession event for outcome-coding purposes — [Goleiro-linha e linha-goleiro: diferentes usos estratégico-táticos do goleiro](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/6816971.pdf)
- In that same study, reception-zone data showed the goleiro-linha most often received the ball still in his own defensive half (47% of plays) or inside the attacking goal area itself (34%), i.e., the GK frequently advances all the way into the attacking box as a receiver — [same source](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/6816971.pdf)
- A separate futsal study on the goalkeeper acting as an outfield player found that teams reduced the size of their defensive area, and reduced the variability of that defensive shape, specifically when the goalkeeper was playing out as an outfield player — framing the tactic's effect primarily as a defensive-subsystem/team-shape metric rather than purely an individual GK stat — [The Influence of a Futsal Goalkeeper as an Outfield Player on Defensive Subsystems in Futsal](https://futsalfocus.net/?p=6972)
- UEFA's technical observers specifically praised individual goalkeepers' ball-carrying and passing contributions in the fifth-attacker role (e.g., Palma's Luan Muller "very fast on the counterattack... as fifth attacker," setting up a goal by advancing with the ball past two pressing defenders before passing) and noted other goalkeepers (Sporting's Guitta, Anderlecht's Roncaglio, Benfica's Gugiel) for similar buildup involvement — but the report gave no numerical pass/shot statistics for these individual performances — [UEFA: Tactical insight — five themes from the UEFA Futsal Champions League](https://www.uefa.com/uefafutsalchampionsleague/news/0281-18065d0d7c90-e6c0ab4ae43e-1000--tactical-insight-five-themes-from-the-uefa-futsal-champion/)

### Inferences
- The available literature suggests two complementary levels at which goleiro-linha "outfield" actions should be tracked: (1) individual possession-event stats identical to any outfield player (passes, shots, losses, fouls) and (2) team-level defensive-shape metrics (area covered, shape variability) capturing the tactical cost of the GK's absence from goal — a teambench indicator set modeled only on individual GK stats would miss this second, team-structural dimension that the academic literature treats as important.

### Gaps
- No source gives individual per-match counts for a specific goleiro-linha (e.g., "completed X passes, took Y shots while playing as 5th attacker in match Z") — the Liga Nacional study aggregates across 183 pooled plays from 10 games rather than reporting individual player profiles.
- The "defensive subsystem" study (futsalfocus.net / shu.ac.uk) was only accessible via a secondary summary; the primary data (how much the defensive area shrank, in what units) was not retrieved.

## How do professional futsal teams/federations (UEFA, FIFA, national federations) or futsal-specific coaching resources describe goalkeeper performance evaluation?

### Takeaway
FIFA and UEFA technical reports evaluate futsal goalkeepers qualitatively and through tournament-level tactical trend statistics (fly-goalkeeper/powerplay deployment and conversion rates, described above) rather than publishing standardized individual GK performance scorecards; coaching resources emphasize a dual-role profile — shot-stopper plus "first/fifth attacker" — as the defining evaluation lens specific to futsal.

### Cited Findings
- FIFA's Futsal World Cup 2024 technical analysis frames the fly-goalkeeper tactic's value in terms of five in-possession objectives, one of which is explicitly "creating numerical advantages" (no further numeric breakdown given for this specific objective) — [FIFA Training Centre: Understanding FIFA Futsal World Cup 2024 in 5 key observations](https://fifatrainingcentre.com/en/game/tournaments/ffwc/2024/understanding-fifa-futsal-world-cup-2024-in-5-key-observations.php)
- UEFA's 2025 Under-19 Futsal EURO technical report states that "modern goalkeepers must contribute with the ball as well as defend the goal," using direct long throws to break pressure and praising Spain's Javi Bule specifically for emerging from goal with the ball as a fifth attacker — [UEFA: 2025 Under-19 Futsal EURO technical report (news summary)](https://www.uefa.com/futsalunder19/news/029f-1f2b7c2a6953-300acec35114-1000--read-the-2025-uefa-under-19-futsal-euro-technical-report/)
- UEFA's 2023/24 Futsal Champions League technical report named a goalkeeper (Palma's Luan Muller) Player of the Tournament, citing his "galactic level of performance" across both legs of the final, his penalty save in the shootout, and his ball-carrying/buildup play as a fifth attacker — treating individual GK recognition as a holistic, narrative judgment by technical observers rather than a published statistical index — [UEFA: Tactical insight — five themes from the UEFA Futsal Champions League](https://www.uefa.com/uefafutsalchampionsleague/news/0281-18065d0d7c90-e6c0ab4ae43e-1000--tactical-insight-five-themes-from-the-uefa-futsal-champion/)
- A UK futsal-coaching resource explicitly defines the futsal goalkeeper's evaluation standard around dual competence: traditional shot-stopping plus being "effectively the first attacker," requiring accuracy with both hands and feet over short and long distances and composure receiving the ball under pressure — [British Football Coaches: What should you know about futsal](https://www.britishfootballcoaches.com/post/what-should-you-know-about-futsal)

### Inferences
- Across both federations, individual futsal GK evaluation in official technical reports is presented narratively (standout performances, named players, qualitative praise) rather than through a disclosed, standardized statistical model — in contrast to the academic multilevel/situational studies cited under the shot-stopping and fly-GK questions above, which do build quantitative models. A teambench indicator set aiming to match "how professional futsal evaluates GKs" would therefore need to synthesize the academic quantitative variables (save %, shot context, fly-GK deployment/conversion) with the federations' qualitative "dual-role" framing, since no single official source publishes a ready-made combined scorecard.

### Gaps
- No full UEFA or FIFA technical-report PDF was directly fetched (only news-article summaries of their findings); any official standardized goalkeeper rating tables those reports might contain (if they exist) were not confirmed or denied by this research.
- No national federation (e.g., Brazil's CBFS/FPFS, Portugal's FPF) coaching or scouting manual with an explicit, published GK indicator list was located within this research pass; the Brazilian material found was academic (university journal studies), not official federation methodology documents.
